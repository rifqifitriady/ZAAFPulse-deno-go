## What ships

- **Route calls on the go engine.** A route is a `router/<name>` config entry with up to 8 targets and a `fallback` or
  `round-robin` strategy. A call whose model is, or maps to, a route is now planned with one leg per target (each a
  complete single-target plan built by today's planner), taken and ordered in `Serve`, and answered by the fallback loop
  of `proxyChat` (and of `responsesPassthrough`), line for line: price filter (402 naming the route), budget filter,
  the token saver once on the client body, then per leg the guardrails of that leg's tier, the call, a 599 or a
  "fell back to the next route target" line, the next leg. Streams pick their leg before the first byte.
- **Classes moved, with the takes JS makes (spec R8, Decision 7):**

  | Class | Takes per call |
  |---|---|
  | chat (whole and stream, all four sub-classes) | 1 |
  | `/v1/messages` (whole and stream) | 2 (the copied double take) |
  | `/v1/messages/count_tokens` | 1 (the first leg decides provider count or estimate) |
  | `/v1/responses` (passthrough and translated, whole and stream) | 2 (the copied double take) |
  | Gemini generate | 1 |
  | Gemini `countTokens` | 0 (as JS; already served) |
  | `/v1/embeddings` | 1 (first allowed leg, no fallback) |

  The engine takes after its rate-limit `Take` and before the price and budget 402s, as `targetsFor` does.
  `GET /v1/models` and `/v1beta/models` stay on js (P4d answer 30, the model cache).
- **The routing functions in Go** (`gw/engine/routes.go`): `resetIn` (with a port of V8's `Date.parse` for the date-time
  formats the providers send), the parts of `readQuota` the cooldown reads (JS `Number()` on strings), `noteResponse`'s
  cooldown arm, `coolingDown`, and `resolveAll` (rotation on the raw names, then resolve, private-only, cooling legs
  moved to the back, stable). One mutex guards the state; the take and the ordering happen under one hold of it.
- **Hand-offs kept** (JS answers as today): a route with no usable target (the 404), and every leg that would be handed
  off (a credential missing, a refresh-grant provider, a body the engine cannot build, an Anthropic leg's translation
  error) hands off the whole call at plan time, before any take, with the reason prefixed `route: `. A route one of
  whose targets resolves with the route's own name (a provider whose id is `router`) is handed off with
  `route: a target named as its route` (final review fix 3).
- **The `routes` switch** (spec Decision 2), as `token-saver` and `provider-auth`: its own engine entry, shadow entry
  and switch-rule record, default `js`. A route call is served on go only when its sub-class **and** `routes` are on go;
  it is compared when either is shadowed (the front shadows chat, messages, responses, gemini and embeddings for a
  shadowed `routes`); shadow lines of route calls carry `route: true`, and hand-offs whose reason starts with `route: `
  count under `routes`.
- **Its place in the switch order:** turn `routes` on after the sub-classes it rides on (`chat` first, then the P4d
  classes, `mcp`, `provider-auth`, `token-saver` as the owner has moved them), because it only acts on calls of
  classes that are already on go. Each switch moves under the owner's rule: 1 clean day and 20 compares in its own
  record.
- **Journal and spans.** The journal opens before each leg's call and closes with that leg's line (599, fallback or
  the answer), so a crash leaves one recovered line for the attempt in flight. Every line becomes its own client span
  under the server span, as `lib/otel.js` makes one per `usage.record`.
- **IPC, additive only** (Decision 14): `provider.note` (both ways), `route.take` (both ways), `route.state` (control
  plane to gateway), schemas and fixtures in `ipc/v1`, and the value `routes` in the `RouteClass` enum of
  `config.push`. No other message changes.

## The replication design (owner Decision 18) and how it was proven

The routing state (rotation counters, cooldowns with their strikes, the quota readings) is replicated both ways, as the
rate-limit window is (P4f D16). Each engine decides synchronously from its own copy, as today, and tells the other:

- `provider.note { providerId, status, at, headers }` for **every** provider answer an engine reads (both answers of a
  401 replay, any class, whatever the `routes` switch says), carrying the observer's clock and only the header names
  `readQuota` reads plus `retry-after`, lower case, values joined as `Headers.get` joins them. The receiver runs the same
  `noteResponse` with `now = at`, so `until`, `resetAt` and the quota's `at` are equal on both sides.
- `route.take { name, at }` for every rotation taken (a remote take is applied only to a round-robin route of more than
  one target, as `applyTake` in JS); takes commute.
- `route.state { rr, cooldown }`, control plane to gateway, sent after `ratelimit.window` and before `budget.snapshot`
  (so before `ready`), so a new or restarted gateway backs off from the same strikes as JS. It carries every cooldown
  entry, expired ones too (an expired entry keeps its strikes until a 2xx clears it).
- A note or take applied from the other side is never sent back. JS stays the store and the dashboard's source for the
  quota records; Go keeps only cooldowns and rotation counters. This closes spec R10 (a provider seen at 429 by one
  engine was invisible to the other) for every class already on go, from the first commit that wires the notes,
  whatever the `routes` switch says.

How it was proven:

- **Function fixtures from the real JS** (`scripts/fixtures/gen-router.js` into `gw/testdata/router`): `fn-resetin`
  (88 values), `fn-readquota` (41 header sets, covering JS `Number()` on hex, octal, binary, exponents, `Infinity`,
  `""`, `-0`, separators), `fn-note` (13 sequences, 57 steps, the state after each), `fn-resolve` (4 sequences, 59
  steps over rotation, cooling and private-only, with a clock that advances per step).
- **Route-level sequences** (`seq-*`, 30 files, 82 client calls sharing one gateway per sequence, so rotation and
  cooldown carry from call to call): chat (rotation of 3, cooldown, fallback on 429, 500, 408 and a throw, all-throw,
  guard block on the second leg, key and provider budgets, private-only, stream fallback, the token saver, an
  Anthropic-format target), messages (dropped fields, model map with round-robin, reasoning drop, saver, stream),
  responses (native, native mapped, translated), Gemini, count_tokens, embeddings, the takes per class, and the
  fallback-then-throw quirk in each client's shape. The Go test replays each sequence through `Engine.Serve` with one
  engine per sequence and compares every provider request, client answer and usage line byte for byte.
- **Shadow fixtures from the real JS** (`shadow-*`, 30 files: each sequence run again with every call marked): 79 route
  calls (102 lines, 13 thrown calls, all eleven route sub-classes) compare `same` as captured under Deno; the quirk
  captured under Node is a `client.body` diff by design (Decision 15f).
- **Spans:** route cases 62–68 in `gen-spans.js` (fallback after 429, throw then 200, stream fallback, an Anthropic
  leg's throw, all threw 502, fallback then guard block, fallback then throw 502), plus a journal check over every
  route sequence.
- **The kill test:** a gateway killed during a route call's second leg leaves one recovered journal line for the
  attempt in flight, and the call's first-leg lines are intact (`test/front-kill.test.js`).
- **The contract proof** (`test/contract/go-routes.test.js`, six tests; `chat` and `routes` on go, `messages` on js,
  against the fake upstream with a second plain-js server for the reference):
  - (a) a go-served chat call gets 429 from provider A; the next js-served `/v1/messages` route call goes to B first
    (one line, not `ipc`);
  - (b) the reverse: a js-served call gets A's 429; the next go-served route call goes to B first (one line, `ipc`);
  - (c) the quota readings of `GET /api/gateway` after a go-served call equal those after the same call in js mode
    (times compared relative to `at`), and are pinned to the expected values;
  - (d) a gateway killed and restarted keeps the cooldown and the counter (`route.state`);
  - (e) round-robin rotation over consecutive go calls equals js mode;
  - and under `ZP_ENGINE=shadow` the same kinds of calls end with the `routes` record compared at least once and
    **diff 0**.

  A mutation check (the control plane's route-event send and `provider.note` apply disabled) made (a)–(d) fail.

## Known differences from the JS gateway (spec Decision 15)

Recorded, not fixed:

- (a) A reset or `retry-after` value with a date shape outside the ported formats: V8's legacy parser reads more forms
  (`+24:00`, ` GMT`, `+0700`, the years 0000–0099 as a guessed day, month and two-digit year) than the port. Kept out
  of the fixtures.
- (b) The one-hop races: a take or a note in flight (about 0.1 ms, P3 measured p95 0.09 ms) while the other engine
  routes. The same as two concurrent calls in one JS process.
- (c) `strikes` when both engines note 429s of one provider within one hop: applied in another order, so the back-off
  of the next 429 may differ by one doubling.
- (d) A route's target count changed by a config push between a take and its echo: the counters may differ by one
  position until the next take.
- (e) An Anthropic leg's whole 2xx that does not convert: the engine's 599 line and 502 say `an: <why>` with Go's own
  text where V8 writes its SyntaxError or TypeError text.
- (f) The cancelled body of the second copied quirk: Node says `Body is unusable: Body has already been read`, Deno
  `Body already consumed`; the engine writes Deno's (the control plane's runtime), and the generator writes the marker
  `<<runtime: body already used>>` so Node and Deno give the same bytes. Under Node the shadow reports it as a
  `client.body` diff.

The shadow reports (a), (e) and (f) under Node as a diff, and (b)–(d) as `routeView: differs` (the go engine's own
counter and cooling set at plan time against JS's first tape entry; reported per class and under `routes` in
`shadow-log.js`, outside the diff gate, since two calls in flight on one route legitimately see different counters).
Also: a thrown provider call is replayed in the shadow as the failure JS named, so Go's own naming of a real network
failure is unproven; the runtime's own naming of a failed fetch stays a known difference outside the shadow.

## JS quirks copied (P4d answer 31 and Decision 7: fixed in both engines after G3)

- **The double take.** `/v1/messages` and `/v1/responses` take twice, so a round-robin route of two targets serves
  those paths from its second target every time; each `GET /v1/models` rotates every route (JS, replicated to go).
  The owner decided (Decision 18) that it stays copied exactly.
- **`/v1/models` rotation** (above): replicated to go through `route.take`, though go does not serve the list.
- **Fallback-then-throw.** When a target falls back (its body cancelled) and every later target throws, `proxyChat`
  returns the target that fell back; the answer path reads its cancelled body, the runtime throws a TypeError, and
  `handle` answers **502 `upstream_error` carrying the runtime's text**, with no line beyond the attempts' 599 and
  fallback lines. Copied in chat (whole and stream), the P4d paths in each client's shape and the Responses passthrough
  loop (`seq-chat-fallback-then-throw`, `seq-xlate-fallback-then-throw`).
- **The shared dropped-features list.** `adaptChat` pushes `reasoning` to the translated body's one `DROPPED` list,
  which every leg shares (an object spread copies the symbol key), so a leg that dropped `reasoning_effort` and fell
  back still names it in the answering leg's `x-zp-dropped` (`seq-messages-reasoning-drop`).

## Deliberately not journalled

Guardrail refusals (a block or failure on a leg) record their usage line but do not open a journal entry, consistent
with P4e Decision 14 and spec Decision 10: a crash leaves no recovered line for a refusal, because the refusal answers
at once and carries nothing to recover.

## Minors left open

From the ledger and the final-review triage (none blocks the merge):

- A prior usage-record copy per leg in the spans replay; the span refusal heuristic in `serve_spans_test.go` is loose;
  `open` before `Start` per leg is asserted only by the kill test; the pre-existing embeddings span emission order
  differs from JS (bytes equal).
- A thrown leg is replayed in the shadow with JS's text; a token-auth route call whose capture holds a 401 is skipped;
  a line recorded after the store is not counted in `sizeOf` (the 64-line cap bounds it); `Capture.Runtime` is
  informational.
- Budget guard and `snapshot.at` in the fixtures use `FIXED_NOW`, not the step clock; `route.take` is sent only for a
  round-robin route with more than one target.
- `applyNote`/`Note` keep entries for provider ids no longer in config (harmless); the route message drain goroutine
  never exits (as the takes'); notes are dropped silently when the 4096-message queue is full (as `ratelimit.take`).
- `Order` panics on a negative `n` (unreachable); `jsTrim` keeps a raw `\xa0` byte; offset-less dates read through
  `time.Local`; no fixture has duplicate headers (`Headers.get` join).
- Contract test: the barrier proceeds when `front.synced` is false only after the 5 s bound (it throws now), and the
  restart case's 15 s cap sits under qm's 30 s cooldown.

## Switching it on (owner)

`gateway.shadow: ["routes"]` compares route calls whatever their class's engine. Once the switch-rule record of `routes`
meets the rule (1 clean day, 20 compares), and the sub-classes it rides on are already on go, set
`gateway.engines["routes"] = "go"`: route calls of every class on go are then served by the engine. Until then every
route call stays on js; the replication of the routing state runs regardless, so both engines already see each other's
cooldowns and rotation.
