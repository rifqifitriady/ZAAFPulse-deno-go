## What ships

- **`/mcp` `tools/call` on the go engine**, behind a new sub-class `mcp` (its own engine entry, shadow entry and
  switch-rule record; default `js`). The engine owns the HTTP edge: the key (scope `mcp`), the rate limit (429 in the
  OpenAI shape), the arguments check, the result check, the usage line (only when JS writes one), the spans (as
  `lib/otel.js` sends them) and the JSON-RPC reply. The tool itself still runs in the control plane: the engine calls
  `POST /_zp/mcp/call` on the internal port as the gateway (`X-Zp-Own` and the signed client header; a client cannot
  reach it), with no time limit of its own and not cancelled when the client leaves. Everything else on `/mcp`
  (`initialize`, `ping`, `tools/list`, batches, notifications, malformed requests, 401/403, a bad
  `MCP-Protocol-Version`) is handed to js, which answers exactly as today.
- **OAuth2 client_credentials and Google service-account providers on the go engine**, behind a new switch
  `provider-auth` (not a path; its own engine entry, shadow entry and switch-rule record, as the token saver's
  `token-saver`). A token-auth call runs on go only when its sub-class **and** `provider-auth` are on go. The engine
  gets its own tokens through `gw/provauth` (the RS256 JWT for a service account), puts them on the request (Bearer, or
  `x-api-key` for an Anthropic-format provider), replays once after a 401 with a forced refresh, and refreshes tokens
  that expire within 10 minutes every 60 s, as `providers.js` does. `secrets.handoff` now carries the stored credential
  of token-auth providers (rule 3: memory only, never logged).
- **Kept on js**: OAuth2 providers with the `refresh_token` grant (Decision 19: two engines rotating one refresh token
  can trigger reuse detection and revoke it), every `/mcp` answer other than `tools/call`, and everything P4d and the
  token saver keep on js.
- **The shadow** compares both: for `mcp` it replays the captured tool value through the engine's checks and reply;
  for token-auth calls it compares the credential header by name and scheme only (each engine holds its own token), and
  skips a capture in which JS replayed after a 401 or its own token fetch failed.

## Known differences from the JS gateway

The spec's list, items 1–10: a failed internal tool call; the Providers page's token expiry shows the control plane's
own token; a change of auth settings fetches a new token at once; two token caches (at most twice the token requests);
tool arguments through `JSON.stringify`/`JSON.parse`; a tool answer the engine cannot parse; a detached tool call at
shutdown past the grace may lose its line; a client leaving during a token fetch; a source replaced once during a
config rollout; the background refresh continuing for a provider whose calls went back to js. Also: a tool-endpoint
answer over 64 MiB gives -32603; log-mode guardrail failures on `/mcp` are marked on the line but not logged by the
engine.

## Suggested switch order for the owner

1. `mcp` once its shadow record meets the rule (1 clean day, 20 compares).
2. `provider-auth` once its record meets the rule; it moves client_credentials and service-account providers of the
   classes already on go.

   refresh).
3. **An OAuth2 refresh-token provider.** Check that its calls stay on js (no `ipc` on its lines; shadow log reason
   "refresh-token grant stays on the control plane") and still answer.
4. **An Anthropic-format provider behind OAuth2**, if the owner has one: the token goes in `x-api-key`.
5. **`/mcp` `tools/call` from Claude Code** against a real integration with `mcp` on go: the tool answers; a secret in
   the arguments is redacted under the default policy and the line carries `ipc`.

## Owner decisions pending (recommended options taken)

Decisions 1–19 of the spec are "recommended, pending owner". The ones to look at: Decision 9 (the service account's
private key and OAuth2 client secrets now reach the gateway process through `secrets.handoff`, as API keys already do),
Decision 19 (refresh-token providers stay on js), and Decision 2 (the tool runs in the control plane, called over the
internal port).
